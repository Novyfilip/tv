Task 1: Channel Analysis

Analysis of four Czech TV channels across ~34,000 timeslots (Jan 2022 – Jan 2024): TV Nova, Prima, Sport Channel, and Nova Cinema.

Share Dynamics

Result: Channel 3 (TV Nova) dominates with the highest average share. Channel 4 (Prima) has the second-highest share at 12.12%, matching Prima's known market position. The Sport Channel has the lowest average share but likely spikes around major sporting events. Channel 54 (Cinema) shows moderate share with high variance; movie performance is inherently unpredictable.

Weekly Trends

Result: The flagship channel shows the strongest seasonal patterns. All channels exhibit some degree of weekly fluctuation, with potential holiday effects visible as spikes.

Hourly Patterns

Result: Strong prime-time effect (19:00–23:00) across all channels, with the main channel benefiting the most. Daytime hours show lower, more uniform shares. This is the single strongest temporal signal for prediction.

Day of Week

Result: Weekend viewing patterns differ from weekdays. The sport channel likely shows the most day-of-week variation due to event scheduling.

Cross-Channel Correlations

Result: Negative correlations between channels confirm the zero-sum nature of audience share; when one channel gains viewers, others lose them. This validates the importance of cross-channel features in prediction models.

Genre Performance

Result: Genre is a meaningful predictor of share. Reality TV and game shows drive high share on Channel 3 (main), while drama and action perform well on the cinema channel. Channel 9 (Sport) has no genre metadata: sport broadcasts don't carry this information.

Data Completeness

Result: Channel 9 is systematically missing features f_7 through f_11 (100% null), consistent with a sport channel lacking movie-style metadata. Other channels have varying levels of missingness that need handling in the modeling phase.
