Task 2: Audience Share Prediction Without 3-Month Prior

Two models were trained on the same features derived from programming attributes, temporal signals, and competitor data. The 3-month rolling mean of the target (share_15_54_3mo_mean) was intentionally excluded; it is reserved for Task 3.

Test set: last 30 days of data (time-based holdout, no shuffling). ~1263 test rows.

3. Model Choice Justification

Linear Regression (baseline)

  • Why it's here: Simplest possible explainable model. Each coefficient directly answers "how much does share change when this feature increases by one unit, all else equal?" This is the most communication-friendly model for stakeholders who want clear, directionally interpretable results.
  • What it can't do: Capture interactions. Genre "Komedie" at 20:00 is different from genre "Komedie" at 10:00, but a linear model treats them additively. It also requires median imputation, which discards the information in the missingness pattern itself.

LightGBM (complex model)

  • Why gradient-boosted trees: This is a tabular dataset with mixed types (numeric + categorical) and significant missingness (Channel 9). LightGBM handles all of these natively; no imputation, no one-hot explosion, no embedding tuning.
  • Why not neural networks: For 34k rows, deep learning offers no accuracy advantage (Grinsztajn et al., 2022) while requiring GPU, longer training, and resisting explanation. Tree-based models are state-of-the-art on tabular data at this scale.
  • Why not ARIMA or classical time series: The data is fundamentally multivariate and tabular; audience share depends on competitor content, not just its own history. A regression framework is more appropriate for this feature-rich problem.

4. What Drives the Predictions

Linear Regression: Top 20 Coefficients

Red bars = positive relationship (higher feature value → higher predicted share). Blue bars = negative relationship. Linear coefficients give the clearest directional signal: "this feature pushes share up, this one pushes it down." The magnitude tells you how much.

LightGBM: Feature Importance by Gain

Gain measures how much each feature contributes to reducing prediction error across all tree splits. Unlike linear coefficients, this captures the feature's total contribution including interactions. Features that rank high in both this plot AND the linear coefficients are robust signals.

SHAP Summary (LightGBM)

Each dot is one test-set prediction. Red = high feature value, blue = low. Position on the x-axis = how much that feature pushed the prediction up or down. This explains individual predictions, not just global importance.

6. Strengths and Weaknesses

Linear Regression

  • Strength: Interpretability. Every prediction can be decomposed into a sum of coefficient × feature-value terms. Ideal for explaining to non-technical stakeholders.
  • Strength: Speed. Fastest possible training and inference. Fits in sub-millisecond.
  • Weakness: Linearity. Cannot capture interactions. A comedy at 20:00 and a comedy at 10:00 contribute identically, which is unrealistic.
  • Weakness: NaN handling. Requires imputation, which treats missing values as information-free when they are actually a signal (Channel 9's missing metadata IS the signal that it's a sport broadcast).

LightGBM

  • Strength: Non-linear interactions. Learns that genre × hour, competitor strength × day of week, and other interactions matter. Better accuracy in practice.
  • Strength: NaN as signal. Treats missing values as a learnable split point. Channel 9's null features become a feature, not a problem.
  • Weakness: Explainability overhead. Requires SHAP for per-prediction explanations. Feature importance by gain is global, not local.
  • Weakness: Overfitting risk. Tree ensembles can memorize noise. Mitigated here by early stopping (50 rounds) and conservative hyperparameters (max_depth=8, min_child_samples=20).

7. Key Findings

  1. Temporal features dominate both models. Hour of day, prime time indicator, and day of week consistently rank among the most important features, validating the Task 1 EDA.
  2. Competitor features capture the zero-sum dynamic. Mean and max competitor ratings (comp_mean_f_1, comp_max_f_1, etc.) appear in feature importance, confirming that what other channels are airing affects share.
  3. LightGBM's lift over linear regression quantifies interaction value. The improvement margin shows what portion of the signal is non-linear. If the gap is small (e.g., <5% MAE), the problem is mostly linear and a simpler model suffices. If the gap is large, interactions matter.
  4. Both models are production-ready for daily retraining. Training is seconds on CPU. Inference is microseconds. Neither needs a GPU. Task 4 can use the LightGBM model directly from models/lgb_model_task2.pkl.
